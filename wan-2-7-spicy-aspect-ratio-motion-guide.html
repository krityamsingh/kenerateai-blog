Wan 2.7 Spicy: Choose the Format Before You Animate

The same source image can behave very differently in vertical, widescreen, and square video. Composition should be planned before motion.

Aspect ratio is not just an export setting. It changes how much room the subject has to move, how camera motion feels, and which parts of the source image remain visible after cropping.

Use 9:16 for vertical short-form content

Vertical video works well when the subject is the main focus and the composition has clear space above and below the face or body. Keep important visual details away from the far left and right edges because those areas may be lost when adapting a wider source image.

Use 16:9 for cinematic movement

Widescreen gives more horizontal space for camera pans, environmental details, multiple visual elements, and lateral movement. It is often easier to create a sense of location or cinematic staging when the frame is wider.

Use 1:1 when flexibility matters

Square composition is useful for feed-style content and for visuals that need balanced space around the subject. It can also be a practical middle ground when the same creative concept will later be adapted into several platform formats.

KenerateAI’s Wan 2.7 Spicy page currently lists 16:9 landscape, 9:16 vertical, and 1:1 square output options alongside 720p and 1080p formats.

Write motion prompts around the frame

  • Vertical: prioritize subtle forward/backward movement and controlled camera push-ins.
  • Widescreen: use pans, tracking, or lateral environmental motion when appropriate.
  • Square: keep the main action centered and avoid excessive edge-to-edge movement.

Start with one main camera instruction and one main subject movement. If the first test is stable, add secondary motion such as background movement, fabric movement, or lighting changes. This keeps troubleshooting easier because you can identify which instruction caused instability.

Preserve room for cropping

If one generated clip may be reused across several platforms, leave extra visual space around the main subject. Tight framing that looks perfect in one ratio can become unusable after reframing.
